# CHAPTER 2: "A BIRTHDAY ON PAPER"

---

The afternoon sun filtered through the forest canopy in shafts of gold, painting the clearing in dappled light and shadow. Birds called in the distance, their songs bright against the warm hum of insects. A gentle breeze rustled leaves overhead, carrying the salt-sweet smell of the ocean.

Arjan sat cross-legged in the center of the clearing, eyes closed, breathing slow and deep.

His hands rested on his knees, palms up. His face was peaceful, serene—but beneath that calm surface, his mind worked with focused intensity.

Around him, hidden beneath fallen leaves and grass, dozens of tiny energy spheres waited. Invisible. Silent. Buried like landmines across the clearing floor.

He'd spent the past twenty minutes placing them, one by one, disguising them beneath the forest's natural clutter. Now he concentrated on keeping them hidden, maintaining just enough awareness to know where each one rested without actually seeing them.

*She's coming,* he thought. *Any moment now.*

The birds went quiet.

Arjan's lips twitched—almost a smile.

*Here we go.*

---

Branches flew from above.

Multiple thick pieces of wood, hurled with precision, aimed directly at his head.

Arjan's eyes snapped open. He rolled sideways in one smooth motion, the branches smashing into the ground where he'd been sitting a heartbeat before.

He came up in a fighting stance—feet apart, knees bent, hands raised.

Lopney dropped from the trees.

She landed in a crouch twenty feet away, then straightened slowly, her pink eyes locked on him. Her twin-tailed black hair swayed with the movement, the ornaments woven into the strands catching the light.

She said nothing. Just watched him with that sharp, calculating gaze.

Then she moved.

---

Lopney came at him fast—faster than seemed possible for someone without powers.

She opened with a leg sweep, low and vicious. Arjan blocked with his shin, feeling the impact jar through his leg. Before he could recover, she was already shifting, flowing into the next attack.

A punch toward his ribs. He twisted away. Another punch, higher. He blocked with his forearm.

She was relentless.

Arjan created spheres around himself—pale blue orbs of energy materializing in the air. He threw them at her, trying to force distance between them, trying to slow her down.

Lopney deflected the first sphere with her palm, batting it aside like swatting a fly. The second she dodged, ducking under it with fluid grace. The third came at her face—she leaned back, letting it pass inches from her nose.

She kept coming.

*Too fast,* Arjan thought, backpedaling. *She's too fast.*

He threw more spheres—a barrage this time, coming from multiple angles. Lopney weaved through them, her movements economical and precise. When she couldn't dodge, she deflected, her hands moving in controlled strikes that sent the spheres spinning harmlessly away.

And she closed the distance.

---

Up close, Lopney was overwhelming.

She struck with practiced combinations—punches, elbow strikes, knee thrusts—each one flowing seamlessly into the next. Arjan tried to defend, tried to create space with his spheres, but she was inside his guard now.

This was her world. Close combat. Martial arts. The thing she'd trained in every single day for as long as he could remember.

She swept his legs.

Arjan stumbled, barely caught himself. She pressed the advantage, driving him backward across the clearing with a flurry of strikes. He blocked what he could, took hits he couldn't avoid.

*I need distance,* he thought desperately. *Just need a second to—*

She didn't give him that second.

A sweeping kick caught his ankle. Another strike to his shoulder knocked him off-balance. He was being pushed back, overwhelmed, outmatched in pure technique.

And then his foot touched the edge of his trap zone.

Arjan smiled.

"Sorry, Lo."

He made a small gesture with his hand.

---

The spheres erupted from the grass around Lopney's feet.

Dozens of them—tiny orbs of energy no bigger than marbles—appearing all at once in a perfect circle. They rose from the ground like bubbles rising through water, surrounding her completely.

Then they detonated.

*Pop. Pop. Pop-pop-pop-pop—*

The sound was like champagne bottles bursting in rapid succession. Each detonation created a small shockwave, not enough to seriously hurt but enough to disorient, to push back, to overwhelm.

Lopney was hit from all sides at once.

The impacts struck her legs, her arms, her torso—tiny explosions battering her from every angle. She staggered, her perfect balance disrupted, her momentum broken.

She fell backward, landing hard on her back in the grass.

Arjan stood over her, breathing hard, proud of himself.

"It's a new technique," he said between breaths. "I hid them while meditating. You never saw them coming."

He grinned. "Pretty clever, huh?"

Lopney didn't respond.

She lay completely still, eyes closed, not moving at all.

---

Arjan's grin faded.

"Lopney?"

She didn't answer.

He took a step closer, concern creeping into his voice. "Hey, Lopney?"

Still nothing.

His heart started to pound. Had he hurt her? The explosions weren't supposed to be that strong, just enough to knock her down, but what if—

"Lo?" He dropped to his knees beside her, reaching out. "Are you okay?"

The moment his hand touched her shoulder, Lopney's eyes snapped open.

She moved like lightning.

Her hand shot out, grabbed his wrist, used his own momentum against him. In one fluid motion she pulled him down and rolled, executing a perfect judo takedown that left him chest-down on the ground with her knee on his back and his arm locked behind him.

Total control. Complete dominance.

Arjan tried to push up. She pressed down harder, keeping him pinned.

"That's not fair!" he protested, his voice muffled against the grass.

She held him for another moment—making her point—then released him.

They both got up, brushing dirt and leaves from their clothes.

---

"That was nasty, Lo," Arjan said, but there was no real anger in his voice.

Lopney dusted off her trousers. "Not my fault. You fell too easily."

"But you seemed hurt!"

"Still," Lopney's voice sharpened, "you shouldn't carelessly approach your enemy, Arjan!"

"You're not my enemy."

The words came out gentle, matter-of-fact. A simple truth between them.

Lopney's expression flickered—frustration mixing with something else. "Still. You're often too naive. If I had your powers I would—"

She stopped mid-sentence.

The words died in her throat. Her face crumpled slightly, the confident fighter's mask slipping to reveal something raw underneath.

Tears slipped out before she could stop them.

She'd been about to say *I'd send some spheres to check first.* But saying it out loud made it real—made her face the fact that she couldn't. That she didn't have powers. That she was always going to be the one without.

That she was always going to need protecting.

Arjan understood instantly. They'd known each other their whole lives. He knew what she was thinking, what she couldn't say.

He stepped forward without hesitation and pulled her into a hug.

---

Lopney tried to hold it together, but the tears came anyway. Frustration. Inadequacy. The feeling of being lesser despite all her training, all her skill.

Arjan just held her, his voice full of love and care when he finally spoke.

"You were brilliant."

She shook her head against his shoulder, but he kept going.

"I'd say you'll have your powers. And if not, I'll become your powers."

Lopney laughed through her tears—a wet, broken sound. "Arjan... You're such a weenie."

"Your weenie," he said with a smile.

She laughed again, this time more genuinely, and they held each other tighter. Finding shelter in that moment, finding hope despite the pain. Brother and sister, completely safe with each other.

The moment stretched, warm and real—

"An interesting display."

---

They jolted apart.

Gallan stood beside them, appearing out of nowhere as if he'd simply materialized from thin air.

"Dad?!" they said in unison, spinning to face him.

Gallan smiled—small, knowing. His long green hair fell across his face, partially obscuring one eye. He looked relaxed, hands tucked in the pockets of his green jacket.

"You certainly did well," he said.

Arjan blinked. "How long have you been there?"

Gallan's smile widened slightly. "I witnessed your technique. Pretty clever, actually."

Then his expression became more serious, though not harsh. "Although you certainly threw it away by going to your sister. That kindness of yours is a liability in combat."

"That's what I told him," Lopney said, wiping her eyes quickly.

Gallan turned to her. "I noticed your fake-out, Lopney. Good instincts."

He paused, letting that compliment settle, then added, "But you should've been more aware of your surroundings. Arjan's spheres were visible if you'd just looked down while you were assaulting him."

Arjan smirked. "Heh, she totally missed it."

---

Lopney's competitive nature flared immediately.

"Still, you've never done that," she shot back. "I felt it."

"Haha. It's just something I came up with while meditating. Pretty cool, huh?"

Lopney smirked now too. "But then who pinned who in the end?"

"Hey, not my fault you cheated."

"That's part of my strategy. Still, you couldn't even hold your own back then."

"How do you know I wasn't luring you backward to my trap?"

"Well, really? I still overpowered you."

"Yeah, but you also fell dramatically backward. That also counts as overpowering, doesn't it?"

They looked at each other, the argument heating up, both refusing to concede.

Gallan watched them with quiet amusement.

"You're both telling the truth," he said. "But—"

He vanished.

---

Both kids tensed, spinning around.

Arjan was already pulling spheres into existence, anticipating where Gallan would appear—

Too slow.

Gallan materialized behind him, already moving. He grabbed Arjan's arm, redirected his momentum, and gently but firmly pinned him to the ground.

"Too slow," Gallan said.

He released Arjan and turned to Lopney.

She was already in a fighting stance, ready for him. They exchanged blows—quick, precise strikes. She actually landed a few, her fists connecting with his arms as he blocked.

But his experience showed.

He caught both her wrists mid-strike, twisted smoothly, and threw her down in a controlled motion. She hit the ground with a grunt.

Gallan looked down at both his students, sprawled on the forest floor, and smiled warmly.

"You shouldn't debate which one is stronger. That's not important." His voice was gentle but firm. "There's always someone stronger."

He extended his hands.

Both kids took them, and he pulled them to their feet.

---

Gallan reached into his pocket and produced two pieces of chocolate—broken from a bar, the chocolate dark and rich-looking.

Rare treat. They both lit up.

He handed a piece to each of them. They ate gratefully, savoring the sweetness.

"Why are you fighting on her birthday?" Gallan asked, genuinely confused.

Lopney looked away, saying nothing.

Arjan answered, his tone playful but slightly protesting. "I wanted to play hide and seek, but Lopney wanted to train."

Gallan's expression softened. "A birthday is a day to be extraordinary."

He reached out, tilted Lopney's face gently so she'd look at him.

"And you're fifteen. That's special for girls."

He leaned in, whispered in her ear: "I've got a surprise."

Lopney's expression was complicated—mildly upset, shame, overthinking as always. She said nothing, just looked down.

Arjan didn't notice the shift in her mood. Gallan did.

"Come on," Gallan said, keeping his tone light. "Let's head home."

---

They walked through the forest together, afternoon light slanting through the trees.

"You're always spoiling my sister, Dad," Arjan said with a grin. "What with all those private training sessions?"

"I teach you everything too," Gallan replied. "You still need improvement."

Lopney walked slightly ahead, quiet.

"And what did you bring my sister?" Arjan continued. "Another book?"

Gallan chuckled. "Maybe."

Arjan moved closer to Lopney, trying to engage her. "I'm looking forward to our rematch."

Lopney smiled despite herself. "You'll lose again."

"We'll see."

The rivalry was affectionate, loving. Lopney's mood lifted slightly as they continued walking, their conversation filling the spaces between trees.

The atmosphere shifted from the serious training ground to something warmer, more familial. No sense of danger. Just family returning home.

The cabin came into view through the trees—warm light glowing from its windows, smoke rising gently from the chimney.

Home.

---

They walked inside, and Lopney immediately noticed the table set in the center of the room.

Three places. Clean plates. Simple but prepared with care.

"Are you going to wear an apron for my birthday, Dad?" she asked, teasing.

Gallan gave her a menacing look—playful but firm. "Don't push your luck."

Arjan laughed.

"Go wash your hands," Gallan said. "Both of you."

They complied without protest, moving to the basin in the corner. Washed their hands in the cool water. Sat at the table.

Gallan disappeared into the back room where he kept the cooking fire.

A moment later he returned, carrying a large dish.

"Happy birthday!" he said, setting it on the table with a warm smile.

The smell hit them immediately—roasted rabbit, perfectly seasoned, surrounded by an abundance of vegetables. More food than usual. Steam rising from the dish.

Arjan noticed Lopney's reaction. "Hey, isn't that your favorite?"

He smiled at her.

Lopney's face lit up—genuine, uncomplicated happiness. "It is."

She smiled back, a real smile, rare and beautiful.

They began eating.

---

For a while there was comfortable silence, just the sounds of eating, the warmth of being together.

Then Arjan spoke.

"You got something like this when you were fifteen, Dad?"

Gallan paused, his fork halfway to his mouth. "I don't think so. My family never had money for these meals. It's why I'm giving it to you."

A brief moment of sadness crossed his face—a flash of memory, gone almost as soon as it appeared. Gloria's face. Her gentle smile. The poverty they'd endured.

Then he ate, forcing the cheerfulness back.

Lopney noticed. She always noticed.

"How was your family, Dad?" she asked carefully. "Do I have grandparents?"

Gallan deflected quickly. "No. We've kind of gone our own ways. Anyway—one, two, three—"

He started clapping.

All three of them sang together: "Happy birthday to you, happy birthday to you, happy birthday dear Lopney, happy birthday to you!"

Lopney smiled despite herself. Arjan grinned wide. The moment was sweet, warm, uncomplicated.

---

"I hope you'll bring me something I like for my birthday too, Dad," Arjan said as they continued eating.

"And what would you want?"

Arjan's eyes lit up. "A God's flag would be nice."

Gallan looked amused. "You do like that race a lot, don't you? You need to see it. I've only brought you pictures."

Lopney interjected, her tone slightly teasing. "He's always dreaming of participating. That's how much he wants to leave."

"Hey, that's not true!" Arjan protested.

Gallan tried to redirect. "I'll have to show it to you. But that's in Kamino. Very far."

"You reckon they'll let us in there?" Lopney asked, her curiosity genuine. "It's Kami's town."

Gallan's face soured at the mention of Kami. A brief flash of pain, of complicated memory. He looked at Arjan—who resembled Kami so much it sometimes hurt—then switched topics quickly.

"Yeah, I think so. Anyway—what else have you been reading, Lopney?"

---

"Nothing of interest as of lately," Lopney said. "Books just don't mention nouryoku at all."

"There's a reason for that." Gallan set his fork down. The warmth in his voice was still there, but underneath it, something quieter. Final. "And it stays that way. You don't talk about it outside this island. Either of you."

It wasn't a new rule. It had always been the rule. But tonight Lopney felt it differently — the way you feel a fence only when you press against it.

"Pristania knew about it," she said.

"Yes."

"And Free."

"Yes."

She waited for more. Nothing came.

Arjan tried a different angle. "Kami didn't hide it though. You always said his deeds were—"

"Great," Gallan said. "They were. He was." He picked his fork back up. "That doesn't mean the world was ready for it."

A pause. Lopney was watching him with that look — the one Arjan never noticed but Gallan always did. The one that meant she was counting things.

"What about Meiji?" she asked.

The table went slightly quieter.

"It's your birthday," Gallan said.

"I know. I'm just asking."

He looked at her for a moment. Then he looked at his plate.

"Some people," he said carefully, "believed Kami was wrong. They followed someone who told them what they wanted to hear." He left it there.

Lopney opened her mouth. Arjan cut in first, his tone deliberately lighter:

"We could start our own. Gallania. Led by Gallan's children."

Gallan let out a short breath — not quite a laugh.

"You'd lose the war in an afternoon," he said.

"Lopney would win it back."

"True," Gallan admitted.

Lopney didn't smile at that. She was still watching him. "You're from Vista Alegre, aren't you."

It wasn't really a question.

Something shifted in Gallan's face — small, fast, gone almost before it arrived. His knuckles pressed together under the table.

"Yes," he said.

"Did you know them? Kami and Meiji?"

"I knew of them." He stood, picking up his plate. "There's more rabbit. Who wants more?"

Arjan raised his hand immediately. Lopney kept her eyes on Gallan's back for a second longer than she should have. Then she looked down at her food.

The question didn't go anywhere. It just sat there between them, unanswered, waiting.

---

He turned to Lopney. "You won your sparring session. Well done."

Lopney smiled a bit, pride showing through.

Then Gallan looked at Arjan. "That trap technique of yours—how did you come up with it?"

Arjan's excitement bubbled up. "I got the idea from hiding. You know, from you."

Gallan smiled gently, pride evident in his expression. He nodded encouragingly.

"I thought," Arjan continued, "if I could hide spheres the way you hide yourself, I could surprise someone. So I buried them in the grass while meditating."

"Clever," Gallan said. Then, quieter: "I've seen someone do something like that before."

His tone had changed. Not much. Just enough.

Arjan looked up. "Really? Who?"

A beat of silence. Short, but Lopney caught it.

"Someone I trained with," Gallan said. "A long time ago."

He didn't offer anything else. His eyes had gone somewhere that wasn't the table.

Arjan, being Arjan, pushed gently. "In Vista Alegre?"

"Yes."

"What was he like?"

Gallan was quiet for a moment. When he spoke, his voice was careful in the way it got when he was choosing what to leave out.

"Talented," he said. "Better than me, for a while." A pause. "We haven't spoken since."

Lopney said nothing. She was doing the arithmetic. The family that had *gone their own ways.* The person from Vista Alegre. The same city. *We haven't spoken since.*

She filed it away.

---

"You know," Arjan said, trying to keep the mood light, "why not just go enter Free HQ and ask them about powers? Maybe they'd know."

Gallan's head turned sharply.

It wasn't a look of mild correction. It wasn't the firm-but-gentle redirection he used when they wandered near something sensitive. It was something older and harder than that — a flash of something he didn't fully suppress before it crossed his face.

Then it was gone. Sealed back behind his eyes.

"That's not funny, Arjan," he said. Quiet. Flat.

Arjan blinked. "I was joking—"

"I know." Gallan picked up his cup. His hands were steady. "Stay away from Free. Stay away from Pristania. I've told you both that enough times that it shouldn't need explaining."

A silence settled over the table. Not hostile. Just heavier than before.

Arjan looked at his plate. Lopney looked at Gallan.

He was already redirecting — posture, expression, all of it — but she'd seen it. That first unguarded second. The way the name *Free* had landed on him like something physical.

Not caution. Not a parent's instinct.

Something that had happened to him.

She didn't say anything. She reached for her drink and let it pass.

---

Arjan, sensing the weight, shifted topics the only way he knew how — forward, lighter, faster.

"So, this technique," he said to Gallan. "Do you have anything in mind with it? Like, could I combine it with—"

Gallan let himself be redirected. He leaned in, asking questions, letting Arjan's enthusiasm carry the room back somewhere warmer.

Lopney listened for a moment.

The conversation about powers was doing it again. Arjan explaining how he'd hidden the spheres. Gallan nodding, engaged, the two of them talking about something she would never have. The person from Vista Alegre who'd had it too.

Everyone had something.
She set her napkin down and stood.
"I need to get out."
Gallan was already pushing back his chair. "Good timing — I'll get started on these dishes." He began stacking plates, movements easy, unhurried. The dinner had run its course and he was glad of it.
"Bathroom," Arjan said to no one, already gone.
Lopney was at the door before either of them had fully stood. Neither of them saw her go.

---

Arjan looked up from his explanation, worried. Gallan watched the door close behind her, his expression difficult to read — something between guilt and helplessness. They'd stirred up things he hadn't meant to stir.

He gave her the only thing he could from across the room.

He let her go.

---

Lopney moved through the forest with aggressive speed.

She ran. Climbed trees. Landed hard, rolled, sprang up. Her movements were sharp, violent, fueled by frustration.

She punched a tree trunk.

Her knuckles split. Blood.

She didn't stop.

Martial arts forms—fast, furious. Kicks, strikes, movements precise but angry. She broke a thick branch with a single kick, the wood splintering with a satisfying crack.

*Why can't I have powers like Arjan?*

She punched another tree.

*Gallan and whoever he trained with both had abilities.*

Another punch. Her knuckles bleeding more now.

*What's wrong with me?*

Kick. Strike. Move. The forms flowed together, muscle memory taking over.

*If I had powers, I could—*

*I'm always going to be the weak one.*

She pushed herself harder, punching until her hands hurt, moving until her muscles burned. The physical pain was easier to deal with than the emotional.

After a while—she couldn't say how long—she stopped.

Breathing harder now. Knuckles bleeding. But not exhausted. Just... done.

She needed to head back. Her family was waiting.

She turned toward home.

---

The paper lay in the middle of the path.

Small. White. Standing out against the dirt and leaves.

Lopney stopped.

Curious. She liked to read. Anything with writing caught her attention.

She bent down and picked it up.

A single kanji character: 罠

She recognized it as kanji but didn't know which one. She studied it, turning the paper over, frowning as she tried to remember if she'd seen this character before.

Five seconds passed.

Ten.

---

Papers exploded from everywhere.

Trees. Grass. All directions.

They rushed at her like a swarm of white locusts, too fast to react, too many to count.

They hit her legs first.

Wrapped around. Stuck tight. She tried to move—couldn't.

More papers on her body, her arms. Layer upon layer, wrapping her like bandages, like a cocoon.

Within seconds she was covered.

Progressive but quick—legs, torso, arms, finally her head.

Her mouth sealed last.

Complete darkness. Can't see. Can't speak. Can't move.

Total panic.

---

She tried to scream. The sound was muffled, useless.

She tried to thrash. The papers held firm, unyielding.

She tried everything she knew—every technique Gallan had taught her, every instinct she'd developed—but it was too sudden, too complete.

She was trapped.

Helpless.

---

Footsteps approached.

Slow. Deliberate. Coming from in front of her.

She couldn't see—only hear.

The footsteps stopped.

A voice spoke. Small, cultured, dripping with amusement.

"How unfortunate."

The voice was close now. Right in front of her.

"The paper clearly said 'trap.' Such inculture."

Footsteps again. Circling her. Examining her like a specimen.

The voice came again, closer to her ear now, menacing despite its small, almost childlike quality.

"There's a punishment to be done."

---

**END OF CHAPTER 2**

**Key Scenes:**
1. Arjan and Lopney's sparring (establishes powers, skills, relationship)

- Dinner exposition restructured around character wants, not reader needs
- "University colleague" replaced with "someone I trained with" — consistent with Pristania recruitment path (no university)
- Gallan's Free reaction made visceral and then sealed — Lopney witnesses both
- Lopney's Kamino/Free theorizing removed — she files things, doesn't broadcast
- Added: Lopney alone at the table, assembling the list she's been keeping
- Added: Vista Alegre book as birthday gift — plants the city as personal to Gallan organically
- Solo training scene: "Gallan's colleagues" → "whoever he trained with" (consistent with revised scene)

- Pristania and Free: Named, felt as dangerous, not explained

- Vista Alegre: Gallan's birthplace, established as a wound
- Nouryoku secrecy: One firm line, not a lecture
- The colleague: Someone Gallan trained with, estranged — the reader doesn't know it's his brother
