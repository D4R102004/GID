# CHAPTER 1: "I'M LEAVING"

---

## ACT 1: ARRIVAL

The island appeared through the morning mist like a promise.

Gallan stood at the bow of the small boat, hands gripping weathered wood. Behind him: the mainland, the world, everything he was leaving. Ahead: dense tropical forest, white sand beaches, cliffs rising from the water like ancient guardians.

No smoke. No structures. No people.

Perfect.

The boat scraped against sand. Gallan stepped onto the beach, his boots sinking slightly. He turned back once—just once—to look at the horizon.

The world he was leaving sat somewhere beyond that line. All of it. The fighting. The orders. The screaming. The blood on his hands that no amount of water could wash away.

He didn't know if they'd come looking for him.

He didn't know if he cared anymore.

Gallan picked up his bag—everything he owned fit inside—and walked into the forest.

---

The clearing revealed itself after an hour of searching.

Hidden beneath the canopy. Close enough to the coast for fresh water. Far enough inland to disappear.

Gallan dropped his bag and looked around. Listened.

Waves in the distance. Bird calls. Wind through leaves.

Nothing else.

"This will do."

His voice sounded strange. Rough. Like he'd forgotten how to speak.

He knelt and pressed his palm against the earth. Solid. Real.

For the first time in years, something in his chest loosened.

Maybe here, he could be something other than what he'd been.

Maybe.

---

## ACT 2: THE ISLAND

*[Four months pass.]*

---

The cabin took shape slowly.

Gallan worked with the precision of someone who'd built fortifications under fire. Measured cuts. Tight joints. Nothing wasted.

But this was different.

These hands — the same hands that had killed, that had erased men from existence and memory — now built something meant to last. Something that wouldn't burn. Wouldn't collapse. Wouldn't be forgotten.

He worked alone. Dawn to dusk. Sometimes past dusk, by firelight, because stopping meant thinking and thinking meant remembering.

Better to work.

---

*[Week 3]*

Gallan paused mid-swing, axe raised above his head.

The dream had come again last night. The same one. Fire and screaming and that look in their eyes when they realized what he was doing. When they knew they'd forget everything. Forget him. Forget what he'd taken.

The axe came down. Wood split clean.

He didn't stop working.

---

*[Week 8]*

The frame stood complete. Gallan stepped back, wiping sweat from his forehead.

Sturdy. Simple. One story, multiple rooms. Nothing fancy.

But it was his. Built with his own hands. No one else's orders. No one else's blood.

He allowed himself a moment of something that might have been pride.

Then went back to work.

---

*[Week 12]*

Gallan sat on the beach, fishing line in the water.

The sun was setting. Orange and gold spreading across the ocean like spilled paint.

He'd caught two fish already. Enough for dinner. But he didn't move.

Just sat.

Watched the waves.

For the first time in longer than he could remember, his hands weren't shaking.

---

*[Week 16]*

The cabin was finished.

Gallan walked through it one final time. Running his hand along the walls. Testing the door. Checking the windows.

Everything solid. Everything right.

He stood in the main room as evening light filtered through the windows. The space felt larger than it was. Empty, yes. But not lonely.

Not yet.

That night, he slept without dreaming.

---

## ACT 3: THE NIGHT

The light came without warning.

No sound. No thunder. No wind.

Just light.

Gallan's eyes snapped open.

The room blazed white. So bright it hurt. So bright he couldn't see anything except brilliance.

He rolled out of bed on instinct — old soldier habits — and hit the floor. Heart pounding. Mind cycling through possibilities in the dark.

But there was no explosion. No heat. No sound of breaking wood or shattering glass.

Just light.

Gallan scrambled to the window and looked out.

Through the gaps in the trees, he could see it: a beam of pure white light descending from the sky, piercing straight through the forest canopy somewhere to the east.

Brilliant. Impossible. Wrong.

It lasted maybe ten seconds.

Then it vanished.

Darkness rushed back. Gallan blinked, eyes struggling after the brilliance. His heart hammered against his ribs.

"What the hell..."

He didn't hesitate. Grabbed nothing — no weapon, no supplies, no time. Just himself.

His power activated without thinking. Old habit. The same reflex that had let him move through rooms full of people who never noticed him, who forgot him the moment he passed.

A deer in the forest looked up as he ran past, then immediately looked away.

Gallan moved fast. Barefoot. Silent.

---

The path led east, toward where he'd seen the beam strike. Four months of living here had mapped the island into his bones. He knew these paths in the dark.

He reached the cave and stopped.

He'd passed it dozens of times. A simple natural formation, nothing remarkable. He'd looked inside once in his first weeks — empty, no reason to go back.

Above it now, a hole gaped in what had been solid stone. Perfectly circular. Edges smooth and unnatural, as if the rock had simply stepped aside.

Starlight fell through it in a pale column.

No scorch marks. No heat. No smoke.

Just the hole, and the silence around it.

Gallan stepped inside.

---

His eyes adjusted to the dimness.

Stone walls. Dirt floor. The hole in the ceiling directly above, maybe two meters across.

And then he heard it.

Crying.

Baby cries.

Two voices — one wailing, high and desperate; the other quieter, but no less insistent.

Gallan's feet carried him forward before his mind had caught up.

Two babies lay on the cave floor directly beneath the hole. Impossibly small. One flailed with tiny fists, face red and scrunched. The other shivered, whimpering softly against the cold stone.

No blankets. No carrier. No sign of anyone else.

Just them.

Gallan dropped to his knees. He looked up at the hole. Then back down at them.

He searched every corner of the cave. Went back outside, circled the tree line, checked the ground for tracks, for footprints, for any sign of another person.

Nothing.

The island was empty except for him.

And them.

He returned to the cave and crouched beside them again, not knowing what else to do.

The louder one — the boy — screamed at the night sky, demanding an answer from it.

The quieter one — the girl — reached out a small hand in the dark.

Her fingers found his.

Gallan went still.

The crying didn't stop. But something in him did.

He looked at her hand around his finger. Then at both of them — cold and small and completely, utterly abandoned on a cave floor in the middle of nowhere, with a hole in the ceiling that no one could explain.

"I don't know where you came from," he said quietly.

It wasn't quite a question. There was no one to ask.

"But I won't leave you here."

---

He carried them home.

The walk back through the forest felt different from every walk he'd taken before. The same path. The same trees. The same dark.

But something had shifted. He couldn't name it. Didn't try.

The babies pressed against his chest, their crying slowly quieting. Finding warmth. Finding something that felt, to them, like safety.

The cabin came into view through the trees, candlelight still glowing from inside — he'd left it burning when he ran out.

Gallan paused at the threshold.

He looked down at the two small faces.

The decision was already made. Had been made the moment the girl's fingers closed around his.

He stepped inside.

The door closed gently behind him, shutting out the night and whatever it held.

Inside: warmth. Light.

A beginning.

---

*[Fifteen years later.]*

## CHAPTER NOTES

**Status:** CANONICAL VERSION — replaces all previous drafts
**Word Count:** ~900 words
**Format:** Prose for personal reading / manga storyboard source

**What this chapter establishes without stating:**
- Gallan is running from something violent (the world, the fighting, the blood)
- He has a supernatural ability — shown through the deer, never named or explained
- He has nightmares about something specific (erasing people's memories)
- The beam of light is not explained — it simply happens
- The babies have no origin — the hole in the ceiling is the only answer, and it isn't one
- The vow — "I won't leave you here" — is the founding promise of everything that follows

**What the reader does NOT know:**
- Gallan's name (not stated until Chapter 2)
- Any faction, organization, or world detail
- What his power is called or how it works
- Who sent the light or the babies
- What Gallan did that haunts him

**The double meaning of the title:**
- Gallan leaving the world behind — the opening act
- Gallan eventually leaving Arjan and Lopney — what Chapter 4 will revisit
