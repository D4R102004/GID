# CHAPTER 1: "THE ISLAND" (REVISED - MYSTERY VERSION)

---

## ACT 1: ARRIVAL

The island appeared through the morning mist like a promise.

Gallan stood at the bow of the small boat, hands gripping weathered wood. Behind him: the mainland, the world, everything he was leaving. Ahead: dense tropical forest, white sand beaches, cliffs rising from the water like ancient guardians.

No smoke. No structures. No people.

Perfect.

The boat scraped against sand. Gallan stepped onto the beach, his boots sinking slightly. He turned back once—just once—to look at the horizon.

The world he was leaving sat somewhere beyond that line. All of it. The fighting. The orders. The screaming. The blood on his hands that no amount of water could wash away.

He didn't know if they'd come looking for him.

He didn't know if he cared anymore.

Gallan picked up his bag—everything he owned fit inside—and walked into the forest.

---

The clearing revealed itself after an hour of searching.

Hidden beneath the canopy. Close enough to the coast for fresh water. Far enough inland to disappear.

Gallan dropped his bag and looked around. Listened.

Waves in the distance. Bird calls. Wind through leaves.

Nothing else.

**"This will do."**

His voice sounded strange. Rough. Like he'd forgotten how to speak.

He knelt and pressed his palm against the earth. Solid. Real.

For the first time in years, something in his chest loosened.

Maybe here, he could be something other than what he'd been.

Maybe.

---

**[MONTAGE: FOUR MONTHS]**

---

The cabin took shape slowly.

Gallan worked with the precision of someone who'd built fortifications under fire. Measured cuts. Tight joints. Nothing wasted.

But this was different.

These hands—the same hands that had killed, that had erased men from existence and memory—now built something meant to last. Something that wouldn't burn. Wouldn't collapse. Wouldn't be forgotten.

He worked alone. Dawn to dusk. Sometimes past dusk, by firelight, because stopping meant thinking and thinking meant remembering.

Better to work.

---

**[Week 3]**

Gallan paused mid-swing, axe raised above his head.

The dream had come again last night. The same one. Fire and screaming and that look in their eyes when they realized what he was doing. When they knew they'd forget everything. Forget him. Forget what he'd taken.

The axe came down. Wood split clean.

He didn't stop working.

---

**[Week 8]**

The frame stood complete. Gallan stepped back, wiping sweat from his forehead.

Sturdy. Simple. One story, multiple rooms. Nothing fancy.

But it was his. Built with his own hands. No one else's orders. No one else's blood.

He allowed himself a moment of something that might have been pride.

Then went back to work.

---

**[Week 12]**

Gallan sat on the beach, fishing line in the water.

The sun was setting. Orange and gold spreading across the ocean like spilled paint.

He'd caught two fish already. Enough for dinner. But he didn't move.

Just... sat.

Watched the waves.

For the first time in longer than he could remember, his hands weren't shaking.

---

**[Week 16]**

The cabin was finished.

Gallan walked through it one final time. Running his hand along the walls. Testing the door. Checking the windows.

Everything solid. Everything right.

He stood in the main room as evening light filtered through the windows. The space felt larger than it was. Empty, yes. But not lonely.

Not yet.

That night, he slept without dreaming.

---

## ACT 2: THE NIGHT

The light came without warning.

No sound. No thunder. No wind.

Just light.

Gallan's eyes snapped open.

The room blazed white. So bright it hurt. So bright he couldn't see anything except brilliance.

He rolled out of bed on instinct—old soldier habits—and hit the floor. Heart pounding. Mind racing through possibilities.

*Attack.*

*They found me.*

*How did they—*

But there was no explosion. No heat. No sound of breaking wood or shattering glass.

Just light.

Gallan scrambled to the window and looked out.

Through the gaps in the trees, he could see it: a beam of pure white light descending from the sky, piercing through the forest canopy somewhere to the east.

Brilliant. Impossible. Wrong.

It lasted maybe ten seconds.

Then vanished.

Darkness rushed back in. Gallan blinked rapidly, eyes struggling to adjust after the brilliance. His heart hammered against his ribs.

**"What the hell..."**

His voice came out rough. Alert.

He didn't hesitate.

Gallan grabbed nothing—no weapon, no supplies, no time. Just himself. He focused his power, feeling it activate like a switch flipping in his mind.

Mental Erasure.

The ability that had made him valuable. The ability that had let him kill without anyone remembering. The ability that now kept him hidden.

Anyone who looked at him would forget. Their eyes would slide past. Their mind would find something else to focus on.

Perfect for hiding.

Perfect for surviving.

He ran out of the cabin barefoot.

---

The forest was dark.

Gallan's eyes fought to adjust after the beam's brilliance. Moonlight filtered through the canopy in scattered patches—barely enough to see by.

His feet found the ground by memory more than sight. Four months of living here had mapped the island into his bones. He knew these paths. Knew the slight rise ahead, the fallen log to step over, the place where tree roots broke through earth like grasping fingers.

His mind raced as he ran.

*What could produce light like that?*

*Natural phenomenon?*

*Something else?*

*Someone looking for me?*

He didn't know. Didn't slow down to figure it out.

Whatever it was, he had to see it. Had to know if his sanctuary had been compromised.

The path led east. Toward where he'd seen the beam strike.

A deer looked up as he passed, then immediately looked away. Its mind sliding past him like water past stone. Mental Erasure making him invisible even to animals.

He reached the cave entrance and stopped.

He'd passed this cave dozens of times during his four months here. A simple natural formation. Stone walls, low ceiling, nothing remarkable. He'd looked inside once during his first weeks—empty, no signs of animal dens, no reason to explore further.

Until now.

Above the cave, a hole gaped in what had been solid stone just hours ago.

Perfectly circular. Maybe two meters across. Edges smooth and unnatural—not shattered, not broken. Cut. Or burned through. Or... something else.

Starlight streamed through it, creating a pillar of fainter light that illuminated the cave's interior.

No smoke. No heat. No scorch marks on the stone.

Just the hole.

Gallan's blood ran cold.

He deactivated Mental Erasure and stepped into the cave.

---

His eyes adjusted to the dimness.

Stone walls. Dirt floor. The hole in the ceiling directly overhead, stars visible through it.

Empty.

Gallan exhaled slowly. Tension bleeding out of his shoulders.

Whatever had happened, it was over. No attack. No—

Then he heard it.

Crying.

Baby cries.

The sound froze him mid-step.

*No.*

*That's impossible.*

He moved deeper into the cave. The crying grew louder. Desperate. One voice high and wailing, another quieter but no less insistent.

And then he saw them.

Two babies lay on the cave floor directly beneath the hole in the ceiling.

Gallan's mind went blank.

They were small. Impossibly small. Newborns or close to it.

One flailed with tiny fists, face red and scrunched with crying. The other whimpered more quietly, shivering against the cold stone.

No blankets. No carriers. No baskets. No sign of whoever had brought them here.

The cave was empty except for them.

And Gallan.

---

He dropped to his knees beside them.

His hands hovered over the babies, uncertain. Shaking slightly.

**"Who—"**

His voice cracked.

**"Where did you—"**

The louder baby's cries intensified. Scared. Alone. Cold.

Gallan looked around frantically. His training kicked in: assess the situation, gather information, determine the threat.

He stood and moved quickly through the cave, checking every corner. Every shadow. Every possible hiding place where someone might be watching.

Nothing.

He exited the cave and circled it completely. Looking for footprints. Broken branches. Disturbed earth. Any sign that someone had been here.

He activated Mental Erasure and extended his awareness, feeling for the telltale gap where another person might be hiding from him.

Nothing.

The babies' cries echoed from inside the cave, growing weaker.

Gallan returned to them.

They were still there. Still crying. Still alone.

No answers. No explanation.

Just two lives, left here to die.

---

Gallan knelt again.

His mind fought itself.

*This isn't my problem.*

Logical. True. He'd come here to escape responsibility, not acquire more of it.

*I came here to be alone.*

He'd built his cabin for one person. Planned a solitary life. This would ruin everything.

*I can't—I'm not—*

Qualified? He'd never raised children. Never even held a baby before. What did he know about caring for someone this fragile?

*They'll die if I leave them.*

That thought cut through everything else.

The quieter baby's hand reached out. Tiny fingers—each smaller than his pinky nail—closed around his index finger.

The grip was weak. Trembling. But determined.

Gallan's breath caught.

The louder baby's cries softened slightly when he leaned closer. His presence apparently some small comfort against the darkness and cold.

He looked down at them.

Two lives.

Two chances.

Two children who would die here if he walked away.

**"I don't know where you came from."**

His voice was quiet. Steady.

The decision already made somewhere beneath conscious thought.

**"But I won't leave you here."**

---

Gallan scooped them up. One in each arm.

Awkward—he'd never done this before, didn't know the right way to hold them. But protective. Gentle despite his callused hands.

They were so small.

How did anyone survive being this small?

The crying quieted. Not completely, but enough.

They pressed against his chest, seeking warmth. Finding safety in his arms despite everything.

Gallan stood carefully. Turned toward the cave entrance.

Paused.

Looked back at the hole in the ceiling one more time.

Starlight streamed through it. Peaceful now. Silent.

No answers there. Maybe there never would be.

He carried the babies out of the cave.

---

The walk back through the forest felt different.

The same path. The same trees. The same island.

But everything had changed.

The babies had stopped crying. Their tiny bodies warm against his chest, trusting despite having no reason to trust.

Gallan looked down at them as he walked.

He didn't know their names. Didn't know where they'd come from. Didn't know who or what had sent that beam of light.

But as moonlight filtered through the trees and ocean waves crashed in the distance, Gallan made a silent vow:

He would protect them.

No matter what came.

No matter the cost.

For the first time since he'd left everything behind, Gallan felt something he'd thought he'd lost forever.

Purpose.

---

The cabin appeared through the trees. Warm candlelight still glowing from inside—he'd left it burning when he ran out.

Gallan paused at the threshold.

Looked down at the children in his arms one more time.

The decision was already made. Had been made the moment the quieter baby grabbed his finger.

But this was the moment it became real.

Crossing this threshold changed everything.

No going back.

Gallan stepped inside.

The door closed gently behind him, shutting out the night and its mysteries.

Inside: warmth. Light. Safety.

A new beginning.

---

**[FADE TO BLACK]**
