# CHAPTER 3: 僵屍

---

## I.

Darkness.

Not the darkness of a closed room or a moonless night — the darkness of something pressed against your eyes. Firm. Unyielding. The smell of paper close enough to breathe.

Lopney couldn't move her arms. Couldn't move her legs. The wrapping went from her ankles to her throat in layers she could feel but not count, each one holding the next in place. She'd tried. Whatever this was, it wasn't coming loose.

She breathed through her nose. Slowly. The way Gallan had taught her when things went bad — *first you breathe, then you think.*

Somewhere in front of her, someone was humming.

Tuneless. Unhurried. The sound of a person completely at ease with their surroundings. She heard the scrape of something being dragged — a chair, maybe — and then the soft rhythmic sound of something sharp being drawn across a surface. Back and forth. Back and forth. A blade being cleaned, or sharpened, or simply handled by someone with a habit.

It stopped.

Footsteps. Slow. Coming closer.

The papers over her eyes softened.

---

## II.

Her neck ached. Her shoulders ached. Everything ached.

The light hit her like a slap. She blinked once, twice, her eyes adjusting — and the cave came into focus around her, and the man standing in front of her did too.

Small. A childlike face that had no business wearing the expression it wore. Yellow eyes catching the candlelight from the desk behind him, wide and bright and deeply interested. He was dressed like something out of a ghost story — traditional robes, wide hat, and on his forehead a paper talisman with the kanji 僵屍 inked in careful practiced strokes. The strokes of someone who enjoyed the writing as much as what it meant.

His head was tilted. He was looking at her the way someone looks at a thing they've been waiting to examine.

He smiled.

"Good evening, sweetheart."

Lopney held his gaze. Long enough to be defiant, not long enough to seem rattled.

"Who are you. What have you done to me."

Not questions. Flat statements — because she already understood what had been done to her, and the question wasn't really about answers. It was about making him speak first.

He clicked his tongue softly.

"My. Those are no manners whatsoever." A small shake of his head, genuinely disappointed. "Ever heard of greetings?"

She kept looking at him. The rage was there if he wanted to find it. The calculation was underneath the rage if he was paying close enough attention.

He was. His smile widened.

"You've got guts, sweetheart." He clasped his hands behind his back and began to move — not pacing, more like strolling, a man with nowhere urgently to be. "But those won't help you now. I politely allowed you to speak and see me for a reason."

"What do you want to talk about."

He stopped mid-step. Looked at her with something that was almost delight.

"Your sharp mind certainly contrasts your lack of culture." A short laugh — genuine, not performed. "Could you not have seen the paper? It clearly said trap. Such inculture. Hahaha."

"Why have you come to this island."

"Straight to business." He sighed — theatrical, fond, as if she were a student who kept rushing past the interesting parts of the lesson. "I've already achieved half my goal. Could that brilliant mind of yours not identify the rest?"

She knew. She'd known since she woke. But she held the blank expression and said: "What do you mean."

"I understand you trying to cover it up." He waved a hand, dismissive. "But stop pretending, sweetie. I know there are two of you." A pause. "I was wondering if you'd tell me about this other child. Is he also illiterate? Probably." He laughed harder at that — head tilted back, yellow eyes bright, genuinely amused by the thought.

Lopney used the pause. Her eyes moved to the desk — maps on the walls, stacks of papers covered in kanji, a knife. And two photographs.

Herself. And Arjan.

Hers had a red cross drawn over it in ballpoint pen. The pen sat on the desk one inch to the left of the picture, as if whoever had drawn the cross had simply set it down and moved on. Finished with that. Ready for the next thing.

Arjan's photograph had nothing on it yet.

*Yet.*

She filed it. Said nothing. Let her eyes move to the hallway at the back of the cave — a low opening, its entrance covered in papers pressed flat and rigid against each other, each one stamped with the character 扉. Door. Whatever was back there, he didn't want her going near it.

She glanced down.

Part of her blouse was gone — torn away from the hem, the section that had been covering her stomach. A clean tear. Deliberate.

She looked back up at him.

Filed it.

Yurisha had been watching her eyes move. He collected the reaction with the quiet satisfaction of a man who had seen it before.

"You've got a keen eye there." His tone shifted — still warm, something underneath it now. "You're not to worry, however. Like I said, I only want to inquire about the other half of Gallan's attempt at family."

The name sat differently than she expected. Not shock — she'd half-anticipated it — but something colder. Confirmation.

"You know my father."

"Who do you take me for?" Genuine offense, briefly. "I would do a job only when I'm fully aware of what I'm facing."

"Then you're not fully aware," she said. "Since you're relying on me to sell my brother to you."

Something flickered across his face. Not anger — more the mild irritation of a craftsman whose work isn't being properly appreciated. He reached into his robe and produced a paper — small, white, the character 刃 inked precisely at its center. He held it loosely between two fingers.

"You know you're not helping your situation." He looked at the paper rather than at her, turning it slightly in the candlelight. "I'm a professional. I enjoy what I do." A pause. "I enjoyed watching you while you were taking your little nap. I let you sleep, you know. Could have woken you up at any time. But you were just too—" he paused, as if genuinely searching for the right word— "what is the word. Cute and tasty."

He licked his lips. Slowly. Deliberately. His yellow eyes didn't leave her face.

The disgust moved through her like cold water. She held his gaze anyway.

"You creep."

"No one is going to hear us here, pinky pie." He smiled at her fury the way you smile at a gift you already knew was coming. "You can scream all you want. The length of time you do it depends entirely on your answers."

She needed time. Arjan would notice she was gone. Eventually. Maybe.

*Stall.*

"Are you a Kiryuin?"

It was the first thing that surfaced — some half-assembled thread from the dinner table, from Gallan's careful non-answers, from the specific way certain names seemed to occupy more space in the air than others.

Yurisha went very still.

Then he laughed — quieter than before. More interested.

"You might not be as illiterate as you look." He studied her with open curiosity now, head tilting again. "But close. I think you've made me like you even more." The warmth in his voice was the most unsettling thing he'd done yet. "However — you ought to know your fate would be no different with them or with me."

"What is your deal with my father."

"Shouldn't you be worried about yourself?" He turned away, moving toward the desk, hands still clasped behind his back. "I merely know he's here. But I won't be seeing him."

"You're scared of him."

He stopped.

The room went quiet in a specific way — the way a room goes quiet when something that was moving suddenly doesn't. A full second passed. Then he turned around, and his expression had gone nonchalant in the particular way that means the opposite — a man choosing not to react because reacting would cost him something.

But then something shifted behind his eyes. A thought arriving. He smiled again, slower this time.

"Trying to buy time, are we." Not a question. "Such feeble taunts only suit immature children such as yourself." He glanced down at the 刃 paper. "Have you not gotten enough proof of the effectiveness of my technique?"

"You're a nouryoku user."

"I can't very well hide that." He looked at the paper, not at her. Then back. "And I do know for a fact—" his voice carried no particular weight on the words, the same tone he'd use to note the weather— "that you are not one."

He moved on immediately. Didn't dwell on it. Already reaching for another thought.

But the words had already landed.

*I know for a fact that you are not one.*

Not an insult. Not a taunt. A fact he'd researched, confirmed, and filed — the same way he'd filed the photographs, the maps, the pen sitting one inch to the left. A door he'd already noted had no lock. He'd moved past it before she could react.

Lopney went still.

Not calculating-still. Just — still. Half a second where the armor wasn't there, where fifteen years of private frustration sat completely exposed on her face, and the cave and the wrapping and the candles all receded and there was just that sentence sitting in the air like something she already knew but had never heard said out loud by someone who didn't care.

Yurisha noticed. Of course he noticed.

His eyes moved to her face and stayed there for exactly one beat — cataloguing, storing — and then he continued walking, already past it, already onto the next thing. As if the moment hadn't mattered. Which was somehow worse than if he'd pressed it.

"So sad to hear it. You being such a beauty in combat, all the same." He turned back to face her fully. "Now. We talk business. I know your brother uses nouryoku. What abilities does he have?"

The half-second was over.

Lopney pulled everything back behind her eyes and said: "How do you know he has and I don't."

The effort it took to keep her voice level — her arms going numb, the dull burn in her shoulders deepening, breathing deliberately shallow to manage the pressure across her chest — didn't reach her words. Yurisha noticed the faint shift in her tone anyway. He always noticed. But he stayed calm, and something in his expression had begun to tire of the game.

She was going to die in a few minutes regardless. He'd decided that some time ago. He found he preferred his targets when they begged. Time to remind her of the terms.

He raised the 刃 paper.

It hardened between his fingers — she could see it change, the texture going rigid and bright-edged in the candlelight, the character 刃 catching the light like a signature.

"I know you're at my complete mercy." His voice had gone quiet. Conversational. "You do know what happens if one of my papers crosses your throat."

He moved his wrist.

The paper crossed her cheek in a single clean motion. Precise. Deliberate. Not deep — a demonstration, nothing more. The cut was a line of heat that became pain half a second later.

Lopney blinked.

That was all. She blinked, and then she looked at him again with the same expression — resolution, not fear. The rage underneath, controlled. Present.

Yurisha looked at her for a long moment.

"You can't act tough," she said quietly. "You don't have the appearance. Nor the methods."

The performance dropped. Not into rage — into something older and quieter and considerably more dangerous. He raised the paper blade and leveled it at her throat, and when he spoke next his voice carried none of the warmth it had held for the last several minutes.

"This is my last warning. What is your brother's ability."

And at that exact moment — at the precise instant the blade was level with her throat and Yurisha's full attention was fixed on her face — Lopney saw it.

A sphere. Floating just behind Yurisha's raised arm. Small. Patient. Waiting.

Yurisha noticed it a half-second too late.

The detonation hit him point-blank. The shockwave threw him sideways and the 扉 papers covering the hallway entrance lost their rigidity all at once, cascading to the floor in a dry rush — and through the opening came Arjan.

---

## III.

He crossed the distance fast and his hands went to her shoulders and he looked at her face — the cut on her cheek, the blood dried at the corner of her jaw — and then down at his own left hand, where he was holding, without seeming to have noticed, a scrap of fabric. Part of her blouse, torn from the hem, the section that had been covering her stomach.

"You okay? I went searching for you and only found strange papers. I saw a piece of your blouse and thought you had been kidnapped. The papers led me to this cave we used to play in and I heard the conversation." He exhaled. "Thank goodness I came in quickly."

Lopney looked at him.

"You silly. He was luring you in." Her voice was flat, not gentle. "He's an assassin. He's planning to kill us both. You could've gone and fetched Dad."

"I tried, but he would not answer. I panicked." A beat. "Is this the thanks I get?"

From across the cave came a sound — resonant, unhurried. Laughter.

They turned.

The papers that had fallen from the entrance were rising. One by one, pressing themselves back against the opening, flattening and hardening — the character 扉 reappearing on each one as they locked into place. Lopney took one step toward it.

The last paper sealed.

Yurisha stood looking at them from across the cave. His right eye was bleeding at the corner but open. Functioning. In his hand was the 刃 paper he had used on Lopney's cheek, still held loosely between two fingers.

"You kids haven't understood that I'm a professional." His voice carried no particular heat. He could have been commenting on the weather. "I killed a number of deer near the path I saw you taking, pumpkin. The smell attracts certain kinds of people." A small pause. "Your father is now investigating a trail of them that leads all the way to the other side of the island." Another pause. Smaller. "He'd be gone for a while now."

"You monster."

Arjan's voice came out quiet. That particular quiet.

Yurisha laughed again. Lopney said nothing — she was already reading the room, measuring distances, cataloguing exits that no longer existed. But first.

"You okay, Arjan?"

"Yeah. Well — surprised." He looked at Yurisha. At the robes, the hat, the talisman on the forehead, the yellow eyes. "Just — the first time we see someone from the exterior in person and it's this." He searched for the word. "Clown? Or are they called performers?"

Yurisha's laugh stopped.

His expression settled into something that wasn't quite amusement and wasn't quite offense. Nonchalant satisfaction, perfectly composed.

"I love my job."

He advanced.

His arm came up and he moved — not fast, not slow, exactly as fast as he needed to be — and the siblings split instinctively in opposite directions. He went for Lopney.

From the desk, every remaining paper rose at once.

They came at Arjan from all directions — not blades, just mass, dozens of sheets hitting his clothes, his arms, his right shoulder, wrapping and pressing and pinning him backward into the cave wall. He tried to pull spheres up. The papers hit his hands before he could focus. He got one arm partially free and then lost it again, and then one sheet caught the skin of his shoulder and he felt the sting of a clean cut opening through his shirt.

Across the cave Yurisha came at Lopney.

She moved — not away, *around* — getting her body off his line and making him adjust. He adjusted easily. The confidence in his expression was total, the self-assurance of someone who had done this many times and knew exactly how it went. But Lopney had spent fifteen years being trained by a man who moved like a veteran, and she'd been watching Yurisha for the last several minutes, and she knew already that he hated being pushed.

She pushed.

She came back at him — inside his range, where the knife was less useful — and made him work for every inch. He worked for it. She could see the faint recalibration in his eyes and she pressed it, forcing him to shift his weight, to change his angle, to stop performing and actually *fight.*

His expression went from theatrical to focused.

Then he threw the knife.

She moved her head. The blade passed close enough that she felt the air of it and a lock of hair drifted to the cave floor. She was already reading his next move —

He'd drawn two.

Both papers hardened simultaneously in his hands, the character 刃 catching the candlelight on each one. His yellow eyes had gone brighter — impressed and annoyed in equal measure, the look of a professional who has had to reach for a second tool.

He came at her with both.

She took one to the wrist — clean, sharp, immediate pain she noted and filed and moved past — and the other she barely deflected with her forearm, and then his guard opened for exactly one moment as he drew back for the next swing and she drove her boot into his stomach with everything she had.

The impact threw him back a considerable distance. He hit the ground hard and slid.

"Ouch."

One word. Flat. Casual.

Lopney stood breathing hard, her wrist bleeding. She turned immediately to Arjan — still pinned against the wall, one shoulder cut, one arm partially free — and crossed to him.

"You okay? Watch those knives."

"I can see them." He finally wrenched his arm fully free, spheres already forming in his palm. "Now watch this."

---

## IV.

Arjan threw the barrage.

Spheres came from his palm in rapid sequence — not pre-placed, not strategic, just volume, as many as he could generate at the man still recovering from Lopney's kick. They crossed the cave fast, aimed at Yurisha's chest, his face, his hands—

Yurisha opened his eyes.

His hands moved — two 刃 papers, one in each fist — and he cut. Left, right, left again. Each sphere split cleanly down the middle before it reached him, the two halves spinning away and dying against the cave walls. The whole barrage lasted perhaps three seconds. When it was done Yurisha lowered his hands and looked at them across the cave with an expression of mild inquiry, as if they'd tried to say something in a language he didn't quite speak.

*Damn it.*

Arjan didn't say it out loud. He didn't need to.

Lopney had already seen past it. While Arjan processed the failure she was scanning — Yurisha's posture, the two 刃 papers, the desk behind him, the walls, the ceiling, the distances between all three of them. Running numbers she didn't have enough of yet.

*What else can those papers do? What haven't I seen?*

Yurisha tilted his head at them. Something almost fond in his expression.

"You babies have nerve." A small sigh, genuinely regretful. "So sad my job today is almost done."

Arjan turned his head slightly toward her, keeping his eyes forward.

"He's onto something," he said under his breath. "We better come up with something ourselves."

"I've got a plan," Lopney said quietly. "I just need to read him a bit better. We don't know everything about his nouryoku yet. If you can distract him with your spheres — keep his attention — maybe I can find something."

"This is not the time to try something." Arjan's voice came out slightly louder than intended, the fear bleeding through. "This guy is going to kill us—"

Yurisha's eyes closed.

Both of them went still.

The cave went quiet — just candles, just breathing — and Yurisha stood in the middle of it with his hands at his sides and his expression completely peaceful, turned inward. Not dismissing them. Concentrating on something else entirely.

Lopney filed it. Shape of the technique. Duration. What it preceded.

The desk's drawers burst open.

Hundreds of papers erupted from them — not 刃, not 扉, something new entirely, each one stamped with the character 拘 — and they were strange, these papers. Not sharp. Tiny, almost. They moved differently from the others, not flying in straight lines but flowing, circling, gathering in slow spirals around Yurisha's now-clenched fists like water finding a drain.

Lopney read the character. Restraint. Binding. Not a blade and not a door.

He stood with them orbiting his hands, his expression back — that particular satisfaction, a craftsman producing his best work.

He burst forward.

They split — instinctively, together — and Arjan pulled spheres fast, trying to get them formed before Yurisha closed the distance—

The kick caught his hands before he could focus. Precise, surgical — not meant to injure, just to interrupt — and it worked. His concentration broke, the half-formed spheres destabilized, and in the moment he stumbled the desk papers rose again, hitting him from three directions, pressing him back toward the wall, one of them opening a clean cut across his shoulder as they pinned him there.

He pushed against them. They held.

Across the cave Yurisha came at Lopney.

She moved — inside his guard, the same pressure that had worked before — and he let her. That was the thing she didn't understand yet. He *let* her come inside, adjusting his footwork just enough to give her the angle she wanted, his expression unchanged, his yellow eye tracking her with the patience of someone who has already decided how this ends.

She pushed him. He absorbed it. Gave ground in small increments — not staggering, not reacting, just *retreating* — and each step she took forward felt like progress and wasn't.

The 拘 papers kept orbiting his fists. Waiting.

She threw a combination — fast, precise, each strike deliberate — and he blocked, redirected, gave another half-step of ground. She was good. He knew she was good. His expression said so — that same impression-and-annoyance from the first exchange, the look of a professional who respects the difficulty of the problem without being threatened by it.

She pressed. He absorbed. Neither of them moved the needle.

Then from behind her — Arjan. A sharp exhale. The sound of someone catching a hit somewhere bad.

One second. Less than one second.

She glanced back.

Yurisha opened his arms.

He moved to grab her wrists — both hands reaching, the 拘 papers already surging forward in anticipation of contact — and she was already turning back, already reading the danger, already too late—

Arjan hit him from the side.

Not a tackle. Not a calculated interposition. Just his body, thrown between them without a plan, his arms coming up instinctively — and Yurisha's hands caught *his* wrists instead of hers.

"Got you."

The 拘 papers didn't care.

They read the contact and came — hundreds of them, hitting Arjan from every direction simultaneously, wrapping his arms, his chest, his legs, his head, layering and pressing and locking with a sound like paper folding in fast-forward, the character 拘 repeating across every inch of him until he was completely, entirely covered. A shape where her brother had been. Still standing for one more second from momentum.

Then not moving at all.

Yurisha looked down at his hands. At Arjan's wrapped wrists between them.

He looked up at Lopney.

"One down." Something almost like warmth in his voice. "One more to go."

Lopney kicked him in the jaw.

The impact drove him sideways, his jaw splitting and bleeding, and he went back several steps — genuinely back this time, not the controlled retreat of before — and Lopney didn't follow. She was already at Arjan, both hands on the papers, pulling, finding nothing. No seam. No edge. No give anywhere.

"Arjan." Her voice came out flat. Controlled. "Can you hear me."

Nothing.

She pressed her palm against the papers over his chest.

Felt it — shallow, already slowing — the breathing underneath.

She tried again to find a way in. Pulled at corners, pressed at layers, got nowhere. The papers were seamless. Purposeful. Designed exactly for this.

Yurisha straightened across the cave, wiping his jaw. His composure was back, the performance reassembled, though the bleeding hadn't stopped.

"I thought you were intelligent enough to know an enemy won't explain his technique." He tilted his head. "Since you're such a beauty in combat — I'll tell you this much. That brother of yours is now dead weight. This is between you and me."

Lopney's hand stayed flat on Arjan's chest. The breathing there was faint. Getting fainter.

Yurisha advanced.

She moved — backward, keeping Arjan with her, one hand still on his chest — barely turning aside the first swing, taking the second across her arm, giving ground because there was no ground left to give strategically, only the ground that existed between her and the wall. Her back found the wall. She was sitting before she understood how, Arjan's wrapped form pulled against her, Yurisha standing over her with his head tilted and his expression soft.

She looked at Arjan in her arms. Thought about throwing him. One clear tactical thought — create space, reset the fight, survive.

She couldn't do it.

Yurisha saw her think it and discard it. Something in his expression shifted — genuine, almost warm, the look he got when a target exceeded his expectations.

"You'll be going with your brother till the end." He raised his hand. "I just like you more now. Farewell—"

Arjan's hand tore through the papers at the wrist.

Not a clean break — a tear, violent and sudden, the 拘 wrapping splitting as something pushed through it from inside. Two spheres were already formed in his palm and they detonated point blank into Yurisha's face before he could register that the hand was free.

The blast threw him hard. He hit the ground and stayed there for a moment, left eye closed, bleeding, not moving.

The papers fell.

Every 拘 sheet dropped away from Arjan at once — instantly, completely, just paper again — drifting to the cave floor around his feet. He stood swaying for a moment, pulling in ragged breaths, and then his legs found each other and held.

Lopney stared at him.

He looked at his own hands. At the papers on the floor. Back at his hands.

"My spheres still worked while those papers were around," he said. Figuring it out as he said it. "They managed to push them off from the inside. I don't—" he shook his head— "I don't know what I did."

Lopney looked at the papers around his feet. At his hands. At the papers again.

"Those spheres can repel the papers?"

"Probably." He exhaled. "I really don't know."

From across the cave a sound tore through the quiet.

Not a groan. A scream.

Yurisha was on his feet — and whatever had defined him for the entire night was gone. The performance, the composure, the cultured warmth, all of it stripped away, nothing underneath but fury and wounded pride and his one functioning eye fixed entirely on Arjan with an intensity that made the cave feel smaller.

"You dumbfounded pipsqueak." His voice shook. "You dare escape my mummification special with some silly spheres." A breath that wasn't steady. "I don't care about the ransom anymore. This is personal. I'll enjoy it twice when I get to slice your throat."

The cave was very quiet.

Lopney's eyes moved to Arjan.

Arjan's eyes moved to her.

*Ransom.*

She filed it. Right beside everything else she'd been keeping.

---

## V.

Yurisha gathered himself.

Not slowly — all at once, the way a fire catches. He was on his feet and the performance was gone. The warmth, the theatrical ease, the cultured delight — all of it stripped away, and what was underneath was simply a man who had been made to look foolish by two fifteen-year-olds and intended to correct that immediately.

His eye found Arjan.

He closed it.

The 刃 papers rose around him — from the floor, from the walls, from everywhere they'd fallen or been thrown — gathering to him in slow orbit, the character 刃 catching the candlelight on each one, dozens of them circling his shoulders and arms until he stood at the center of a slowly turning constellation of edges.

Then he opened his eye.

Lopney and Arjan stood shoulder to shoulder, both bleeding, both breathing hard. She looked at him — really looked — and the plan assembled itself from everything she'd filed across the last hour. The 拘 papers needed contact. The 刃 papers needed his focus to aim. Every technique took something from him. And when something interrupted that something, the papers fell.

Arjan could survive close range. She couldn't climb with him watching her.

"Here's what I'm thinking," she said quietly, not looking away from Yurisha. "He's past calculating. He'll take less notice of his surroundings than before."

"Okay." Arjan was watching too. "What do you need?"

"Entertain him. You're immune to his mummification. I need him focused on you." A pause. "Can you do that?"

Arjan looked at the orbiting papers. At the single functioning eye fixed on him across the cave. "I don't think we can approach him as he is. Those blade papers are dangerous."

"I know." She kept her voice level. "Trust me."

He looked at her for a moment. Confused. Uneasy. Then something settled in his expression — not certainty, just the decision to trust her despite the absence of it.

"Okay." He took a breath. "I'll give him a piece of pain."

He rushed forward.

Yurisha's eye opened fully and the 刃 papers responded immediately — a volley cutting outward at angles Arjan had to read and dodge in the same motion. He caught one on the knee, another on the right arm, hissed and kept going, closing the distance with two spheres already formed in his hands—

Yurisha cut them both.

One knife in each hand, precise and fast, and the spheres split before they could detonate. Arjan threw more. Yurisha cut those too. They were close range now — too close for the blade papers to be useful, not close enough for Arjan's spheres to land before they were cut — and Yurisha was superbly composed despite the missing eye, his depth perception adjusted, his movements efficient and relentless. He pressured. Arjan gave ground and came back. He pressured again.

Arjan dodged the first throat swing.

He dodged the second.

The third came in low — a feint, and Arjan read it wrong, correcting for a blow that wasn't coming — and the paper knife caught him in the left lung. Not deep. Exactly as deep as Yurisha intended.

Arjan's air left him all at once.

He didn't stumble — his legs held, just — but the hand went to his side and stayed there and everything slowed down for a moment, the cave and the candles and Yurisha's face all receding slightly while his body decided whether it was going to keep going. Blood on his lips. On his chin. On the cave floor beneath him.

He coughed once.

Yurisha watched him flinch. And then he stopped.

One moment — just one — where his eye lost its focus and went somewhere else entirely. Somewhere years away. Arjan's face in that specific configuration — the pain, the effort to stay upright, the refusal to go down — had pulled something out of storage that Yurisha hadn't accessed in a long time. A room he'd forgotten he had. A face that looked like this one, in different light, on a different day, at the end of something that had changed everything.

"You." His voice came out strange. Quiet. Not his assassin voice and not his performance voice — just a man asking a question he didn't have the framework for. "Who are you?"

Arjan threw both spheres directly at his face.

The explosions were weakened — Yurisha had already started pulling his focus back, already half-recovering before they hit — and the blast was enough to push him back but not enough to put him down. He steadied himself. The strange moment was gone. What replaced it was simpler and older and considerably more dangerous.

He swung.

Arjan blocked and took the hit anyway. Another swing — arm. Another — shoulder. Yurisha moved through him methodically, no performance left in it, just the efficient application of damage, and Arjan didn't back away because backing away wasn't the plan. He kept his feet. He kept his hands moving. Spheres came from his palms — not thrown, just generated, accumulating in the space around Yurisha's body while he took the hits that kept coming, one after another, each one adding to a total that his body was keeping track of even if his mind refused to.

His expression didn't break.

Yurisha pulled back for the next sequence and Arjan looked at him across the small distance between them — breathing badly, one hand on his lung, blood on his chin — and smiled.

"Heh." His voice was quiet. Certain. "You're going down."

Yurisha looked at the spheres surrounding him.

The detonations came simultaneously — from every angle, small and precise, a constellation of force closing in at once. The cave filled with light and sound. Yurisha moved — and the 刃 papers he'd thrown at Arjan during the exchange came back, boomeranging on his instruction, cutting through several of the spheres before they could fully detonate. The blast was halved. Maybe less.

The smoke cleared.

Yurisha was still standing.

His hat was ruined — the paper talisman torn almost completely away, the first character gone, only 屍 remaining against the scorched brim. His robes were shredded at the shoulders, the chest. His jaw was still bleeding from Lopney's kick. His left eye was closed and dark.

He looked at Arjan.

Arjan looked back. One hand pressed hard to his side. Breathing in careful increments. Spheres depleted. The smile still there, but smaller now — the honest face of someone who had spent everything and found out it wasn't quite enough.

Yurisha raised his arm.

One paper blade. That was all he needed. He stepped forward—

She came from above.

No warning. No sound of movement. Just suddenly a body in the air, falling backwards from the cave roof with one leg extended, dropping with everything she weighed behind it — and the kick connected with the back of Yurisha's neck at the precise point where velocity and gravity and fifteen years of training arrived at the same moment.

The sound it made ended things.

Yurisha's face hit the cave floor. His nose. His mouth. The paper blade fell from his fingers. The 刃 papers dropped around him all at once — every one of them going soft and still and just paper — and his body followed the rest of the way down and did not move again.

The cave went quiet.

Lopney landed beside him and stood for a moment, making sure. He wasn't moving.

Then her legs gave out from under her and she sat down hard on the cave floor, one hand wrapped around her bleeding wrist, breathing in long unsteady pulls. Across from her Arjan was already sliding down the wall — one hand still pressed to his side, lung still wrong — until he was sitting too, the ruined hat lying between them and the unconscious shape of Yurisha.

They looked at each other.

Neither of them said anything. Just breathed. The candles on the desk still burned. The cave smelled of smoke and blood and old paper.

Arjan exhaled — long, unsteady, ending somewhere near a laugh.

"Fiu." He looked at her. "You were great, Lo. I was cooked there."

"I told you I had a plan." She leaned her head back against the cave wall. "Don't I always have a plan?"

"Haha." He looked at the ceiling. "But I weakened him for you."

"You sure did." A pause. "You also lost the one-on-one."

"His one-on-one with me certainly went better than yours."

"Oh, what are we doing. This is silly."

"I love you."

She reached across and found his hand and held it. They stayed like that — both on the floor, both bleeding, the unconscious Yurisha between them and the way out — and said nothing else for a while.

The candles burned.

## VI.

The 扉 papers were still harmless.

They lay flat where they'd fallen, just white rectangles with 扉 inked on them, and the cave was quiet now in a way it hadn't been quiet before — the candles lower, the smoke settling, the papers and the blood and the ruined hat all exactly where the fight had left them. Lopney and Arjan sat in the middle of it not speaking, their hands still linked, when footsteps came through the entrance.

Gallan came through at a pace that wasn't quite running but was everything running was except the speed. His long green hair was loose, his jacket open, and his face carried an expression Arjan had never seen on it before — not the controlled sternness of training, not the warm deflection of dinner, not the careful neutrality he put on when conversation moved toward things he didn't discuss. This was something older and rawer than any of those. This was a man arriving somewhere he was terrified to arrive.

He saw them.

He crossed the cave in four steps and came down to his knees between them and his arms went around both of them at once — not gentle, not careful, just completely — and held them there without saying anything at all.

Neither of them spoke either.

Then he pulled back just enough to look at their faces. Arjan first — the blood on his chin, the hand pressed to his side, the careful incremental breathing. Then Lopney — the cut on her cheek, the wrist held in her own fingers, the exhaustion sitting deep behind her eyes.

"Are you okay. What happened."

"This hitman was sent here to kill us," Lopney said. Her voice came out steadier than she expected. "They were apparently targeting us specifically."

Gallan's eyes moved to Arjan.

"He said he'd been offered ransom." Arjan's voice came out slower than usual, the lung making everything harder. "We beat him, Dad. I tried to call you but you were nowhere."

"I got scared." A pause that held something heavier than the words. "I smelled blood in the woods near the path and went to investigate. I was a fool. You should've been my priority."

"It's okay, Dad." Arjan tried to sit up straighter and gave up halfway. "I came to save Lopney. I saved her."

"I saved you too," Lopney said. "Don't."

Arjan started to respond. She coughed — once, sharp, the kind that came from somewhere deep — and stopped him.

Gallan was already moving. His hands went to Arjan's side first, pressing carefully around the lung wound, reading it with the efficiency of someone who had done this before many times in places far worse than this. Then Lopney's wrist. The cut on her cheek. Not gentle — thorough.

"You'll explain everything properly later," he said quietly. "Right now I need to get you both home to the medical kit."

"You can't leave him, Dad." Lopney's voice had gone quieter. "He's the hitman. His papers—"

"I know." Still not looking up. "I'll deal with him. You did beautifully. Both of you."

He said it the way he said things that were simply true.

By the time he finished assessing them both Arjan's eyes had closed — not sleep, unconsciousness, the body making the decision his mind wouldn't. His breathing was shallow but steady, his head tipping against the cave wall.

Lopney lasted a little longer. She was watching Gallan with that particular expression — assembling, filing, waiting — and then her eyes closed too, the wrist still held in her hand, her head falling forward slightly.

Gallan looked at them both for a moment.

Then he stood and turned toward Yurisha.

He crossed to him and crouched and pressed two fingers to the side of his neck. The pulse was there — weak, unsteady, but present. He kept his fingers where they were for a moment longer than necessary.

Then he looked at the face.

He stayed very still.

It wasn't a long moment. Just long enough for something to move through his expression that had no name and no audience — his children unconscious behind him, the candles burning, the cave quiet — and then it was over. Whatever it was, it went somewhere behind his eyes and did not come back out.

He reached into Yurisha's robes. Twenty 刃 papers — he counted them as he pulled them out, folded them once, put them in his jacket pocket. Then the papers from the shelves. Then the desk papers. He stripped the cave of everything.

Then he moved the desk over Yurisha's body.

He stood. Looked around the cave once without expression. Then he went to his children.

Arjan first — he lifted him carefully, one arm under his back and one under his knees, and Arjan's head found his shoulder without waking. Then Lopney — she stirred slightly when he lifted her, her fingers tightening once around nothing, then going slack.

He carried them both out of the cave, one at a time, and laid them carefully on the grass outside. Activated his ability. Stood for a moment in the dark island air, the forest around him exactly as it had always been, the ocean somewhere through the trees.

Then he gathered them up again — Arjan over one shoulder, Lopney over the other — and walked home.

The cabin was dark when he arrived. He lit the lamp, laid them both down, and went to the medical kit on the shelf without pausing.

He worked in silence. The lung wound first. Then the cuts, the wrist, the cheek, everything accumulated across the night. The lamp made the cabin warm and small. Outside the island was quiet.

When he was finished he sat back and looked at them — both of them bandaged, both of them breathing, both of them alive in the warm light of the cabin he had built with his own hands fifteen years ago — and he pressed his knuckles together and held them there, and said nothing.

## VII.

Yurisha woke up under a desk.

This took him a moment to establish. His awareness returned in pieces — the weight across his chest and legs first, then the dark, then the smell of candle wax gone cold, then the pain. The pain came last and all at once and was considerable.

He lay still and took stock.

Neck — damaged. Every attempt to turn his head sent white light through his vision. Ribs — at least two, possibly three, the right side of his chest a single continuous complaint. His left eye was gone for now, swollen completely shut, and the right was blurred at the edges. His nose had been broken cleanly on the cave floor and had since stopped bleeding, which was something.

His papers were gone.

He noticed this the way you notice a missing limb. Every surface he could reach — robes, floor around him, the underside of the desk — nothing. Stripped. All of it.

The kanji for 刃 was in his muscle memory. He reached into the lining of his robes, found the paper he kept there for exactly this kind of situation, and wrote the character by feel in the dark.

It hardened between his fingers.

He cut through the desk above him. One half fell. He screamed — couldn't help it, the ribs — and then lay still again, breathing through it, until the screaming stopped feeling necessary.

Then he moved.

Dragging first. Then crawling. Then something between crawling and walking that wasn't quite either. He left the cave and found the forest and kept going, one hand against a tree trunk when he needed it, the island air warm against his face, the path toward the shore somewhere ahead.

His mind, when it wasn't occupied with the mechanics of moving, went to familiar places.

*Kyouhei Shinichi. The legendary Yurisha. Beaten by children.*

He pushed through the undergrowth. A branch caught his shoulder and he hissed and kept going.

*Children with a nouryoku, he corrected himself. That was relevant. The boy's spheres repelling his 拘 papers from the inside — he had never encountered that. Nobody had told him. Nobody had thought to mention it because nobody knew.*

*It was a new technique. Undeveloped. The boy himself didn't understand what he'd done.*

He would recover. He would return to his work. There were things he would do, once he had his health back, once he'd had time to think about the boy with the spheres and the girl with the kick and the father who had stripped his papers while his children lay unconscious and had not left a single sheet—

The voice came from the night.

"Where are you going, Shinichi?"

Yurisha stopped.

The forest was dark around him. No shapes. No movement. Just trees and the distant sound of the ocean and a voice that had no body yet, coming from somewhere he couldn't identify.

He knew the voice.

A room came back to him — a chair, a table, a bag of coins set down with the particular carelessness of someone for whom the amount was trivial. An odd grin. Eyes that found things funny that weren't funny.

*"There's a job that needs your doing, Shinichi. It shouldn't be too difficult. Two birds, one knife wielder."*

His pulse was doing something it shouldn't.

"I'm merely set back." He kept his voice level. Professional. He was a professional. "The job isn't done yet. I can still—"

Laughter.

It came from somewhere above and to his left and then from somewhere ahead and then from everywhere, the way a sound fills a space when the space is the right shape for it. Not loud. Just present.

"Why weren't you able to carry out the deed you were asked for?" A pause that enjoyed itself. "HAHAHAHAHA."

Yurisha's hand found the last paper in his lining. His fingers closed around it.

"I have information," he said. "The boy's ability — I know how it works now, I saw it, if you want them taken properly you need to know what you're facing—" He heard himself and stopped. He was bargaining. His voice was doing things he hadn't authorized it to do. "I can even deal with Gallan if you want. No extra payment. Just—"

"You've failed."

Flat. Final. The laughter gone as if it had never been.

"I'd say the bells toll for you now, assassin."

A pause.

"HAHAHA."

Yurisha looked down.

The grass around his feet was not quite right. Small distortions in the dark — shapes that didn't belong there, ovals of something slightly brighter than the surrounding night, lying in the grass in a loose constellation around him, patient and still.

He recognized the geometry a half-second before it mattered.

The night turned red.

The ellipses detonated in sequence — not all at once, one after another, a fraction of a second between each, so that each one arrived before the sound of the last had finished. They were very precise. They were very thorough. The sound they made was quiet, considering what they did, each small and surgical, the whole sequence lasting perhaps two seconds.

Then the forest was dark again.

And still.

## VIII.

Several minutes later, Gallan found the place where Yurisha had been by the blood trail.

He'd left the kids sleeping, made sure of their breathing, and activated his ability at the door before stepping back into the night. The cabin behind him — locked, dark, safe for now. Ahead — the island, and whatever it held.

He moved fast and silent through the forest, reading the ground as he went. The trail wasn't hard to follow — whoever had been moving through here had been moving badly, one hand against the trees, leaving evidence at every step. He followed it through the undergrowth toward the shore path, toward where a small vessel was presumably still docked at the water's edge.

The trail ended in a clearing.

Gallan stopped at the edge of it.

The clearing was wrong. The grass was flattened in a pattern that had a shape to it — not the shape of a struggle, not the shape of a fall. Something else. Something that had come from multiple directions at once and left the ground scorched in small precise circles, each one the size of a large stone, distributed across the space in a way that suggested planning rather than chaos.

No body.

He walked the perimeter. Checked the treeline, the ground, the undergrowth beyond. Whoever had been here was gone — completely, deliberately gone, taking everything with them. No trail leading out. Nothing left behind.

Almost nothing.

He almost missed it.

It was lying half under a root at the edge of the clearing, caught there as if it had been carried a short distance by the force of something and come to rest against the wood. A hat. Wide-brimmed, traditional, the kind that belonged to a specific costume and a specific profession and a specific man whose face Gallan had looked at in a cave an hour ago and whose pulse he had checked with two fingers pressed against his neck.

He crouched down and picked it up.

The paper talisman was still attached to the brim. Barely — the edges were scorched and torn, most of it gone. What remained was a single character, the first one lost entirely, only the second surviving in the damage.

屍.

Gallan held the hat in both hands and looked at it for a long time.

The clearing was quiet around him. The ocean moved somewhere through the trees. Above the canopy the stars were out.

He thought about the scorch marks in the grass. The pattern of them — small, precise, distributed with intention across the clearing in a shape that wasn't random. A nouryoku he didn't recognize. Someone who had known where to find Yurisha on this island tonight, who had the ability and the foresight to remove the body afterward, and who had left nothing except this — a burned hat, a single surviving kanji, and marks in the grass that told him nothing except that whoever made them was considerably more dangerous than the man who'd been wearing this hat.

He stood.

He put the hat under his arm.

He walked home.

The cabin was warm and still when he came back through the door. Both of them breathing — he checked, because he always checked — Arjan's chest rising and falling in careful increments, Lopney's hand still loosely wrapped around her bandaged wrist.

He went to his room.

He put the hat on the shelf at the back, behind the things that were in front of it, where it wasn't visible from the door.

Then he sat on the edge of his bed in the dark and pressed his knuckles together and held them there.

Outside the island was the same as it had always been. The same trees. The same ocean. The same dark.

But something had found them here.

And whatever it was, it had been watching long enough to know exactly when to arrive.
