# **KENSUKE SUGIMORI / PROTOS - COMPLETE CHARACTER PROFILE**

---
ROLE: Supporting (Free leadership) → Tragic Hero (Arc 2 death)
FACTION: Free (Co-founder, Acting President)
STATUS: Dies Arc 2
AGE_ARC_1: ~60s (same generation as Kami)
---

---

## **BASIC INFO**

**Full Name:** Kensuke Sugimori (杉森健介)
**Nickname/Codename:** Protos
**Age:** ~60s (Arc 1-2) — born roughly the same era as Kami
**Occupation:** Acting President of Free; former Pristania 4 Emperor
**Status:** Co-founder of Free, Rajin's husband, Meiji's adoptive father, Lyzander's adoptive grandfather. Dies Arc 2.
**Family:**
- Rajin / Megami (wife — he alone knows her true identity)
- Meiji (adoptive son)
- Lyzander Sugimori (adoptive grandson, carries Kensuke's surname)

---

## **NICKNAME SIGNIFICANCE**

**Protos (πρῶτος — "First/Primary"):**
- Derived directly from his Nouryoku — he creates protective bubbles, he is the one who **shields**
- "The first protection." The one who stands before harm reaches others.
- Fitting for a man whose defining act in life is his final one: casting an indestructible bubble around his son before dying.
- Also implies primacy — he was there from the very beginning of Free, before anyone else.

---

## **PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION**

**Build:**
- Tall for his age — still carries the frame of a fighting man
- Not brawny, but solid. A veteran's body: lean, worn, enduring.
- Moves with the deliberate economy of someone who has never wasted a step in his life.

**Face & Hair:**
- **Blonde hair** — somewhat long, parted and pointed at the ends. Not wild, but not neat. Has a timeless, slightly archaic quality to it.
- **Full blonde beard and moustache** — well-kept, pointed. A man who takes quiet pride in his appearance.
- **Black eyes** — sharp, still, penetrating. The kind of eyes that assess a room before entering it.
- **Expression:** Rude at first glance. A default severity that reads as hostility until you know him.
  - Look deeper: the lines in his face aren't from anger. They're from endurance. A man who has carried a great deal for a very long time.
  - There's something of the old snake charmer about his face — patient, watchful, certain of himself in a way that unsettles people who expect old men to be uncertain.

**Distinctive Features:**
- **Rings on his arms** — worn on both forearms. Not decorative in an ostentatious way. They've been there long enough to feel part of him.
- **Scepter-claw weapon** — his instrument of close combat and his primary tool for launching Protos Spheres. An elegant, slightly archaic weapon. Feels old. Feels like *his*.

**Clothing:**
- **Yellow trousers** — the most vivid thing about him. A deliberate choice.
- **Grey traditional garment** on top (hanfu/similar East Asian-style layered cloth) — muted, practical, dignified.
- **Brown and grey accents** throughout — earthy tones that frame the yellow without competing with it.
- **Overall impression:** Strategic, not ceremonial. This is a man who dresses for function but has opinions about it. The yellow reads as an old signature — something he wore before he was famous and never changed. He doesn't need to announce himself.

**Body Language:**
- **Default:** Still. Economy of movement. Doesn't gesture unless it means something.
- **In conversation:** Direct eye contact — the kind that makes younger people look away first.
- **In combat:** Unhurried. The most dangerous thing about Protos is that he never seems rushed.
- **With Meiji:** The stillness softens marginally. He doesn't perform warmth, but it's there — in the slight relaxation of the jaw, in the way he holds silence differently with his son than with anyone else.
- **With Rajin:** The only time he looks like a younger man. Whatever he felt for her at forty, he still feels at sixty. He doesn't show it publicly. But he doesn't hide it either.

**Overall Impression:**
- An old soldier who never stopped being one.
- The kind of man you'd follow without needing a speech.
- Patient, cold, and unexpectedly capable of love.

## **PERSONALITY CORE**

### **Defining Traits:**
1. **Strategic and cold** — thinks first, speaks second, acts third. Rarely wrong.
2. **True ideological believer** — not performative. Free's ideology is the marrow of who he is.
3. **Ruthless when necessary** — was more brutal than Meiji during the 3-Year War and would be again if asked.
4. **Privately loving** — the coldness is genuine, but it doesn't go all the way down. His love for Rajin and Meiji is the exception that proves the rule.
5. **Unforgiving toward Kami's legacy** — the betrayal at Pristania calcified into something permanent. He doesn't harbour grudges. He simply doesn't forget.
6. **A straight man** — no theatrics, no speeches. He says what he means once. He doesn't repeat himself.

### **Core Values:**
- Free will and human agency — not as abstraction, but as something he watched be crushed and decided never to allow again.
- Loyalty to the people he has chosen, not to institutions.
- Protecting what matters — his family, his ideology, the organization he built.
- Honesty, even when it costs something.

### **Fatal Flaw:**
- **Coldness as self-insulation.** Kensuke's emotional distance was built as protection and became a wall. He watched Meiji grieve Kami's death and felt joy, and couldn't bridge that gap. He tried — not with words, but with presence, with small acts, with patience. He was never quite able to say *I am glad you are my son* the way Meiji needed to hear it. He ran out of time.

### **What Makes Him Happy:**
- Rajin. Simply: Rajin.
- Free functioning as it should — Meiji leading, people living without fear of Pristania.
- A good fight, handled cleanly.
- The rare moments Meiji laughs at something.

### **What Enrages Him:**
- Pristania. Not abstractly — specifically, personally. The memory of what they did and what they stood for.
- Sentimentality over Kami. He has no patience for it — not in Meiji, not in anyone.
- Weakness in the form of inaction. He never confused grief with weakness, but he had little patience for paralysis.

### **What Haunts Him:**
- The distance that grew between him and Meiji after Kami's death.
- That Rajin is gone so often. He understands why. He misses her anyway.
- The suspicion, never voiced, that he pushed Meiji toward something Meiji wasn't ready for — and that the damage may be permanent.

## **VOICE & DIALOGUE**

### **Speech Patterns:**
- **Economical.** Every sentence has a purpose. He doesn't fill silence.
- **Direct to the point of bluntness** — not cruel, but uninterested in softening things unnecessarily.
- **Formal register by default** — the speech of a man who came up in Pristania's hierarchy and never quite lost the cadence.
- **Warmth shows in restraint** — when he's being kind to Meiji, you notice it because he says slightly *more* than usual.
- **No irony, no wit.** That was never his currency.

### **Sample Dialogue:**

**To Meiji (seeing him withdrawn):**
> "You're still at this."
> *(Not a question. Not a reprimand. Just: I see you. I'm still here.)*

**To Free leadership (strategic meeting):**
> "We don't negotiate from fear. We negotiate from position. When we have one, we'll talk."

**To Rajin (private moment):**
> "I'd rather you be difficult and present than easy and gone."

**On Kami's death (to Meiji, who is grieving):**
> "He was a man who believed the world owed him agreement. It didn't. That's all."
> *(The most comfort he knows how to offer. It isn't enough. He knows.)*

**Final words to Meiji (Arc 2 death scene):**
> "I'm sorry I couldn't say it better. You were worth everything we built this for."
> "Live on. For Free. For what we made."
> *(Dies before Meiji can answer.)*

### **Verbal Habits:**
- Refers to Meiji simply as "Meiji" in professional contexts — never "son" in public. The formality is its own kind of respect.
- In private: occasionally "boy" — not diminutive, more like something ancient and affectionate that slipped through.
- Never raises his voice.

## **BACKSTORY**

### **Origins — Pristania (Early Life):**

**Joined young:**
- Came to Pristania in his youth, drawn by Kami's ideology of absolute morality.
- He believed it. He was young enough to believe things completely.
- Rose through the ranks — eventually reached the 4 Emperors, the elite tier directly under Kami.
- He was approximately **38 years younger than Arc 1** when he left — so mid-20s when he walked away, roughly.

**The Disillusionment:**
- Kensuke watched Kami's ideology from the inside and reached a conclusion: it was not righteousness. It was control dressed as righteousness. Authority disguised as morality.
- He confronted Kami directly — a full quarrel. He called it what he saw: authoritarianism. The enforcement of a single man's moral vision on everyone else, without consent, without recourse.
- **Kami's response** is what stayed with Kensuke: Kami listened. Understood. And let him go — with the dignity and restraint of a man genuinely convinced of his own righteousness, who saw no need to cage someone who disagreed.
- This is the part Kensuke cannot forgive more than the ideology itself. Kami was not a villain in that room. He was a true believer who simply could not see that true belief, enforced on others, becomes tyranny.
- Kensuke left enraged. Not at being released — but at the knowledge that Kami would never understand why the release was insufficient. That a man who *lets you leave* as an act of righteousness has already missed the point entirely.
- He left swearing to bring Kami's organization down. Not from hatred — from conviction. The same fire that drove him to Pristania originally now drove him away from it.
- He left before the duel. Before Meiji was raised. He saw what was coming before most people acknowledged it.

**What He Carried Away:**
- A permanent, bone-deep contempt for Kami's ideology.
- The combat skills and strategic formation of an Emperor.
- And — eventually — Rajin.

---

### **Rajin / Megami (His Wife):**

**How They Found Each Other:**
- The specifics TBD. What is known: they found each other in the years between Kensuke's defection and Free's founding.
- They were united first by ideology — both had reason to oppose Kami. Then by something more.

**The Secret He Holds:**
- Kensuke is the **only living person** who knows Rajin is Megami — Kami's ex-wife, one of the three Ryakujin prototypes, biological mother of the Kamigami.
- He has held this secret for decades.
- He does not hold it with strain. He holds it with **pride** — the pride of a man trusted with the most important thing in the world by the person he loves most.
- He keeps it because she asked him to. That is enough.

**Their Dynamic:**
- Rajin travels. She is often absent. Free HQ is sometimes more a waypoint for her than a home.
- Kensuke doesn't resent this — he understands who she is and what she carries. But he misses her.
- When she is there, the coldness in him recedes slightly. Not fully — it's part of him now. But slightly.
- He loves her in the way certain people love: not loudly, not with performance, but with absolute constancy.

---

### **Raising Meiji:**

**The Project — and The Person:**
- Kensuke and Rajin raised Meiji with explicit purpose: to oppose Kami. They taught him ideology from childhood. They shaped his worldview deliberately.
- Kensuke does not regret this. He believes they were right.
- But Meiji became a person — not just a purpose. And somewhere in there, Kensuke became a father, not just a strategist.

**The Duel and Its Aftermath:**
- When Meiji killed Kami, Kensuke felt genuine joy. Relief. The vindication of everything he had believed and sacrificed.
- Meiji felt horror.
- That gap — Kensuke rejoicing, Meiji grieving — was never fully closed. Not for lack of trying. Kensuke tried to lift his son's spirits every time he saw him foundering. He sat with him. He stayed. He didn't understand the grief, but he honoured it by refusing to leave.
- It made him feel, sometimes, like a man speaking a language he learned too late.

**The Distance:**
- The relationship became complicated not from hostility but from incomprehension.
- Kensuke is incapable of mourning Kami. Meiji is incapable of not.
- They love each other entirely. They simply cannot fully reach each other across that divide.

## **POWERS & COMBAT**

### **NOURYOKU: PROTOS SPHERES**

**Ability Name:** Protos Spheres (informal; others call them "Kensuke's bubbles")
**Nature:** Generation and manipulation of indestructible protective barriers

---

### **How It Works:**

**Sphere Generation:**
- Kensuke generates translucent spheres — roughly human-sized, though variable.
- A person inside a Protos Sphere is **completely invulnerable** to external harm while the sphere holds.
- The sphere can be maintained indefinitely as long as Kensuke wills it — and, crucially, **as long as he lives.** (See: Death Mechanic below.)

**Offensive Application:**
- Spheres can be **launched as projectiles** — propelled by his scepter-claw weapon.
- When weaponized, a sphere has the mass and impact force of a giant bowling ball.
- He can materialize multiple spheres and manipulate their trajectories.
- Opponents can **interact with the spheres physically** — push them, redirect them — which Kensuke uses tactically, turning the enemy's own force against their positioning.

**The Scepter-Claw:**
- His weapon of choice for close combat and sphere manipulation.
- Elegant, archaic design — somewhere between a ceremonial scepter and a fighting tool.
- Used to strike, redirect, and launch spheres with precision.
- Also effective as a standalone melee weapon. He is not a man who relies on his Nouryoku alone.

**Martial Arts:**
- Trained in close combat from his Pristania years.
- Doesn't lead with it — his default is spheres and positioning — but he is capable, and opponents who underestimate an old man tend to regret it.

---

### **CRITICAL MECHANIC — DEATH AND THE LIVING SPHERE:**

**The Vital Rule:**
- A Protos Sphere is sustained by Kensuke's living will.
- Normally, if the user dies, their Nouryoku effects cease.
- **Kensuke's Arc 2 death is the exception that proves — and hints at — a larger rule.**

**What Happens:**
- At the climax of Arc 2, Kensuke casts a Protos Sphere around his captured son Meiji.
- The sphere holds even after Kensuke dies.
- Pristania cannot kill or harm Meiji. The bubble simply does not break.
- Kensuke dies. The sphere remains.

**What This Means for the Story:**
- This is the first visible hint to the reader that **certain Nouryoku can persist beyond the user's death** under specific conditions.
- It subtly foreshadows the nature of Kami's power — the way 40% of it remained sealed in his corpse after death, rather than dissipating.
- The audience should feel the resonance without being told it directly.

---

### **Combat Philosophy:**
- **Support-first, control-oriented.** Kensuke does not fight like a brawler. He fights like a strategist.
- His ideal position in any conflict: *protected* (inside a sphere), *mobile* (scepter in hand), *surrounded by options* (multiple spheres in play, environment controlled).
- He fights to outlast, not to overwhelm.
- In his prime, he was feared. Not because he was the strongest, but because fighting Kensuke meant fighting someone who had already planned three exchanges ahead.

**Strengths:**
- Invulnerability (both personal and for allies)
- Battlefield control via sphere manipulation
- Tactical patience — nearly impossible to bait into recklessness
- Scepter-claw mastery for close range

**Weaknesses:**
- Not a high-damage fighter — he holds and controls, doesn't destroy
- Sphere generation requires concentration; sustained heavy combat fatigues him at his age
- The spheres can be used against him if redirected cleverly by a fast opponent
- He is old. His body is not what it was.

## **KEY RELATIONSHIPS**

### **Rajin / Megami (His Wife):**
*See Backstory section above.*
- The one person who can make him look like a younger man.
- The secret he holds with pride and longing in equal measure.
- Her absences are the quiet wound in his life. He never mentions it.

---

### **Meiji (His Adoptive Son):**
- Raised him as a weapon and watched him become a person.
- Loves him in the way men who don't know how to say it love: by staying, by showing up, by sitting in the room even when there's nothing useful to say.
- The grief between them (Kensuke's joy vs. Meiji's guilt over Kami) is the central wound of their relationship. Kensuke tried to close it. He ran out of time.
- Calls him "Father" or "父上" formally. Kensuke never corrected this. Secretly preferred it to something more casual — it meant Meiji took him seriously.
- **His death is for Meiji.** Everything at the end is for Meiji.

---

### **Lyzander (His Adoptive Grandson):**
- Carries Kensuke's surname: Sugimori.
- Kensuke told Lyzander about the Kami duel when Meiji couldn't bring himself to.
- Emotionally distant at times — not from indifference, but from the same coldness that runs through everything Kensuke does.
- Lyzander's grief partially stems from this distance. Kensuke recognizes this and doesn't know how to fix it. He tries, occasionally, in small ways.

---

### **Free (The Organization He Built):**
- Not loyal to Free as an institution — loyal to what it means.
- Was more ruthless than Meiji during the 3-Year War. Matched the Kamigami's brutality without flinching.
- Distrustful of the peace treaty (signed 15 years before Arc 1). Saw it as compromise dressed as victory.
- Never stopped preparing for the war he believes is coming.
- When it does come — in Arc 2 — he is ready, and he dies on his terms.

---

### **Pristania / Kami (Former Organization):**
- No nostalgia. No residual loyalty.
- What he took from Pristania: his skills, his strategic education, and a permanent understanding of what authoritarian morality looks like from the inside.
- Rejoiced at Kami's death. Has no complicated feelings about it.
- The Kamigami are enemies — not abstractions of enemies, but real and present ones.

## **ARC-BY-ARC ROLE**

### **Arc 1 (A Wondrous Island):**
- Not present in the main story.
- **Behind the scenes:** Acting President of Free. Kensuke (as possessed by Shiro) appears at the end of Arc 1 — searching Gallan's house, making the phone call the kids overhear, leading them unknowingly toward Free HQ.
- The reader does not yet know who he is. He is a silhouette. A voice through a window.

---

### **Arc 2 (The Land of the Free):**

**The Possession — 5 Years Running:**
- Shiro has been inside Kensuke's body for the entirety of Free's island period.
- Kensuke has been **conscious the entire time** — aware, trapped, unable to act.
- He watches Shiro operate his body, make his decisions, speak with his voice.
- Occasionally he tries to resurface. These attempts are brief, involuntary, and suppressed.
- **For the reader:** These moments of attempted resurfacing are intentional story hints — something is wrong with "Kensuke." A flicker. A stutter. Something that doesn't quite fit.

**The Revelation:**
- At the climax of Arc 2, the possession ends.
- Shiro has served his purpose — Free HQ's location has been delivered to Pristania.
- Before exiting, Shiro deliberately inflicts a mortal wound on Kensuke's body.
- Shiro returns to Reiji's body. Kensuke regains control of himself — for the first time in five years.

**The Death:**
- Kensuke is mortally wounded. He knows it immediately.
- Meiji has been captured. Pristania is preparing to act.
- Kensuke, with what remains of his strength, casts a **Protos Sphere** around his son.
- Pristania cannot touch Meiji. Cannot harm him. The sphere holds.
- Kensuke gives a final speech to Meiji — brief, direct, entirely characteristic. He apologizes for the distance. He tells Meiji it was worth it. He tells him to live on. For Free. For everything they made.
- He dies. The sphere remains.
- **This is a shocking moment for Meiji.** The man who could not always say it out loud said it at the end.

## **THEMATIC ROLE**

### **What Kensuke Represents:**

1. **The Cost of Certainty**
   - Kensuke was never wrong about Pristania. Never wrong about Free's purpose. Never wrong about the war.
   - His certainty cost him the ability to fully reach his son.
   - Being right is not the same as being present.

2. **Love as Action, Not Language**
   - Everything he did for Meiji was love. He simply couldn't translate it.
   - His death is the one sentence he got exactly right.

3. **The First Generation**
   - He was there before Free was anything — before the name, before the duel, before the myth.
   - His death closes a chapter of the world. Meiji is now alone in a way he wasn't before.

4. **The Lore Hint**
   - His persisting sphere after death plants the seed for the reader: *power doesn't always die with the body.*
   - This will matter later.

## **WRITING KENSUKE — QUICK REFERENCE**

### **Dialogue Do's:**
✅ Short, direct sentences. No filler.
✅ Calm even when the situation isn't.
✅ Warmth through restraint — says slightly more than usual when being kind.
✅ Formal register. No casual banter.
✅ Factual about Kami — no hatred in the voice, just flat certainty.

### **Dialogue Don'ts:**
❌ Long speeches (that's not him)
❌ Emotional outbursts — ever
❌ Self-pity
❌ Doubt about Free's ideology
❌ False warmth — he doesn't perform what he doesn't feel

### **Action Beats:**
✅ Still, economical movement
✅ Scepter-claw as extension of his arm (natural, not showy)
✅ Spheres launched with efficiency, not drama
✅ Eye contact that holds until the other person looks away
✅ The slight jaw-relaxation around Meiji (his tell for warmth)

### **Internal Thoughts (When Narrated):**
✅ Strategic — always reading the room
✅ Longing for Rajin (quiet, constant)
✅ Frustration at his inability to close the gap with Meiji (rarely acknowledged directly)
✅ Absolute contempt for Pristania (cold, not hot)
✅ Pride in Free — genuine, uncomplicated, the one thing that never wavered
